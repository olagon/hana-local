The models

hana-local reads your text with a small open-source language model running on this device over WebGPU. Bigger models read better and need more memory and a stronger GPU. Smaller ones fit lighter hardware but miss more, so lean on the review step. The default ships with the app. Picking any other model downloads it once from a public CDN, then it too runs offline. Switch any time with the picker under the paste box, and your choice is remembered.

Whichever model you pick, everything runs here on your device. Sizes are one-time downloads, not ongoing usage.

Your words never leave this device

The model runs here, on your machine. Your tasks are stored here. Your words are never uploaded, and no one, including us, can read them.

How that is true

hana-local has no server, no account, and no sync. The language model that reads your pasted text runs inside your browser on your own hardware, using WebGPU. The text you paste is handed to that local model and nowhere else. Your tasks live in your browser's local storage (IndexedDB) on this device only.

Nothing to download

The default model ships with the app: its weights are plain files sitting next to this page, served from the same place. Beyond the analytics counts above, opening hana-local fetches nothing from anyone else. If you switch to a different model in the picker, that model downloads once from a public CDN, then works offline from then on. It is a plain file download, the same for everyone, carrying none of your text.

What we count, and what we can't see

hana-local uses Google Analytics to understand usage: how many people visit, which model they run, and how many tasks get added. Those are numbers only. Your words are never sent to Google, to us, or to anyone. That covers the text you paste, task titles, notes, projects, and dates. Like any web request, Google receives standard connection details such as your IP address, and a content blocker will block it without affecting the app. Offline, nothing is sent at all.

Move your data

Because there is no cloud, the backup and transfer path is a plain file that only you hold. Export writes every task, with its date, project, notes, done state, and Holding Tank state, to a JSON file. Import reads a file back: it adds tasks you don't have and updates the ones you do, field by field, keeping your current value for anything the file leaves out. Import a newer file into an older copy to bring it up to date.

How hana-local works

hana-local turns whatever you paste into a clean task list. Drop in a full email, meeting notes, or a single line like "call the vet Friday" and press Make tasks.

Adding tasks

A small language model running on this device reads your text, pulls out the real tasks, and shows them for a quick review. Uncheck anything that doesn't belong, edit titles, dates, and projects right there, then press Add. Pressing Enter accepts everything at once. If something you paste matches a task you already have, hana-local updates that task instead of making a duplicate.

Dates

New tasks land on today unless your text names a deadline. Tap any date pill to change it. Dates never change on their own. Anything past its date gathers under a Late heading at the top of the list, tinted so it stands out, until you finish it or give it a new date.

Active and the Holding Tank

Two lists sit behind the tabs at the top. Active is what you are working on now or soon. The Holding Tank is a backlog for someday, on hold, or waiting-on things. Both keep whatever due dates you set, and nothing moves on its own. Move any task with → Tank or ← Active, and when hana-local reads your pasted text it sorts clear backlog items straight into the tank for you.

Everyday use

Tap the circle to finish a task. Finished tasks rest in the Pau section at the bottom of the Active tab. Tap a title to reword it, tap + project to group it, and use the project chips to filter. The × removes a task for good.

The model

hana-local runs a small open-source model in your browser with WebGPU. The default, Qwen2.5 3B, ships with the app and starts loading the moment you open the page, so it is ready before your first paste. The switch-model picker under the paste box trades speed for accuracy. The 3B models read best. The smaller ones fit weaker devices and phones but miss more, so lean on the review step. Switching to a non-default model downloads it once from a public CDN right then, with a progress bar, and your choice is remembered. The models page up top describes each one.

Talk instead of type

The paste box is a normal text field, so your device's own dictation types right into it. On a Mac, turn on Dictation in System Settings under Keyboard. On Windows, press the Windows key and H. On a phone, tap the microphone on the keyboard. Dictation is your device's own feature and follows your device's privacy settings.

Move your data

Export and Import live on the privacy page. Export saves every task to a file. Import reads one back, adding what's new and updating matches so their status and dates follow the file. Since the file wins on a match, import a newer file, not an older one.

Keyboard

⌘↵ or Ctrl↵ runs Make tasks from the paste box. Enter accepts a review. Escape closes this page, the privacy page, or a review.
